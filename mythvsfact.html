Fact vs. Fiction: Social Media Myths

This page collects common colorectal cancer myths shared online and compares them with evidence-based facts from trusted public health and medical organizations.

Fiction: "Coffee enemas can cure or prevent colon cancer."

Fact: There is no scientific evidence that coffee enemas cure, treat, or prevent colorectal cancer. They are not endorsed by any major medical organization and carry real risks, including serious infection, electrolyte imbalances, bowel perforation, and death. The FDA has warned against their use.[1][2]

Fiction: "An alkaline diet can prevent or cure cancer by changing the body's pH."

Fact: The body tightly regulates blood pH within a narrow range (7.35-7.45) regardless of what you eat. Food does not meaningfully alter blood pH in healthy individuals. No credible scientific or clinical evidence supports the idea that an "alkaline diet" prevents or cures cancer.[1][4]

Fiction: "Supplements sold online can replace colorectal cancer screening."

Fact: No dietary supplement has been shown to detect or prevent colorectal cancer. Screening tests (colonoscopy, FIT, stool DNA tests) are the only evidence-based tools for early detection. The FDA does not approve supplements for cancer prevention purposes.[1][3]

Fiction: "If you have no symptoms, you don't need to worry about colorectal cancer."

Fact: Early-stage colorectal cancer and precancerous polyps often cause no symptoms at all. By the time symptoms appear - such as rectal bleeding, unexplained weight loss, or a change in bowel habits - the disease may be more advanced. This is exactly why screening before symptoms is recommended starting at age 45 for average-risk adults.[1][2][3]

Fiction: "Colorectal cancer only affects older people - young adults don't need to worry."

Fact: Colorectal cancer is now the leading cause of cancer death in men under 50 and the second leading cause in women under 50 in the U.S.[3] While average-risk screening begins at 45, younger adults with symptoms, a family history, or other risk factors should speak with a healthcare provider promptly. Delays in diagnosis among younger adults are common precisely because of this misconception.

Fiction: "Red meat is fine - only processed meat is actually dangerous."

Fact: Both are classified as cancer risks by the IARC. Processed meat (bacon, hot dogs, deli meats, sausage) is a Group 1 carcinogen - meaning there is sufficient evidence it causes colorectal cancer in humans. Unprocessed red meat (beef, pork, lamb) is classified Group 2A - meaning it probably causes colorectal cancer. The evidence for processed meat is stronger, but the recommendation is to limit both.[5]
